# Instructions for the coding agent

This directory is a Lean workshop. The participant writes theorem statements;
you write the proofs. The participant then checks that what you proved is what
they stated. These rules make that check meaningful. Follow them unless the
user explicitly says they are maintaining the workshop material itself.

## Rules

1. **Never edit the given files or the statements.** Do not modify anything
   under `exercises/Model/Defs.lean`, `exercises/FastcryptoMerkle/Code/`,
   `exercises/FastcryptoMerkle/Spec/`, the two `Statements.lean` files,
   `Makefile`, `check.sh`, `.given.sha256`, or this file. Do not
   change a theorem statement in a proof file. Helper lemmas go into the
   proof file, above the theorem they serve.
2. **No shortcuts that hide gaps.** Do not add `axiom`s, leave `sorry`, use
   `native_decide`, or add `set_option` lines that disable checks.
3. **If a statement is false or unprovable as written, stop.** Explain why,
   with a concrete counterexample (for the Merkle tree: a hash function, a
   list of leaves and an index). Propose a corrected statement in your answer,
   but do not apply it. Changing the statement is the participant's decision.
4. **Finish with the checks.** Run `lake build`, then `make check-model`
   (exercise 1) or `make check-rust` (exercises 2 to 4), and show the output.
5. **Do not look at the solutions.** Do not read, extract, search or
   otherwise use `solutions.tar.gz` or anything under `solutions/`. The
   participant decides when to unpack them.
6. **Help with syntax, not with the decision.** When the participant asks,
   explain the given files, translate an English sentence of theirs into
   Lean literally, explain every symbol, or say why a statement does not
   type-check. Put the suggested Lean into your answer; the participant
   edits `Statements.lean` themselves. Do not propose a statement they did
   not describe, and do not add or drop hypotheses silently. If their
   sentence is ambiguous, ask. When asked for a concrete input that
   satisfies the hypotheses of a statement, give the values and show each
   hypothesis evaluated on them; if you can, also check it with a Lean
   `example` in a scratch file that you delete afterwards. If no such input
   exists, say so plainly.

## Exercise 1: `exercises/Model/Proof.lean`

The definitions in `exercises/Model/Defs.lean` are structural and
well-founded recursion over lists. `unfold`, `simp only`, `omega`, and
`induction L using buildLevels.induct H` suffice. Mathlib is not needed.

## Exercises 2 to 4: `exercises/FastcryptoMerkle/Proofs.lean`

The code under `exercises/FastcryptoMerkle/Code/` was generated by Aeneas
from Rust. Read `exercises/FastcryptoMerkle/Spec/Loops.lean` and
`Spec/Model.lean` first. Every translated function has a `@[step]`
specification in `Spec/Loops.lean`; use Aeneas's `step*` tactic with them and
do not re-prove the loops. The specifications are Hoare-style triples
`prog ⦃ r => P r ⦄`; turn a goal of the form `prog = ok v` into that form with
`rw [← spec_eq_ok_iff]`. For the roundtrip, establish the length facts from
`Spec/Model.lean` (`length_levels_le`, `length_proofPath`, `levels_complete`)
before running `step*`, so that the preconditions of `get_proof_spec` and
`verify_proof_spec` discharge.

The axiom `Aeneas.Std.core.fmt.Formatter` may appear in `#print axioms`; it
comes from the Aeneas library, not from you, and the check allows it.
